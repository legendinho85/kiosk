# Six options for our series hero

*Made Happy, 24 September 2026. **For discussion, and to be finalised with the founder.** The evidence behind these options is in [`character-research.md`](character-research.md).*

All six are friendly, round and teddy-like. None is a bear or an otter, and each is designed to look unlike any famous character. In every option the **child is the hero** of the job day (named, never drawn) and the character is their best friend who comes along.

> **Names are provisional.** This session ran out of web searches before the name checks could run, and the search sites are blocked here. Every name below has been checked **only against my general knowledge**, so treat it as **unverified** until we run the checks listed in [Name checks still to do](#name-checks-still-to-do).

---

## My recommendation

**First choice: Mallow, a cuddly toy lamb who comes to life.** It follows your own reasoning most directly: most children have a cuddly toy, and Mallow *is* one, without being a bear. No jobs series is led by a toy. Mallow also carries a sewn name label on one foot, so **the child's name appears on the hero in every book** (through the app, and later on a real Mallow soft toy). And every story gets a built-in bedtime ending: Mallow goes back to being a cuddly toy.

**Close runner-up: Nimble, a baby elephant.** This is the strongest *real* animal. It has an unmistakable silhouette, a trunk that can do every job, and no existing jobs series. If you'd rather have a real animal than a toy, pick Nimble. (The toy-come-to-life idea could also be used with an elephant.)

**Next step:** get quick rough sketches of Mallow and Nimble (plus the bunny as a "popular" control). Show them to 5–10 parents of 2–5-year-olds: "Which would you buy for a three-year-old?" Then clear the winning name. After that we agree the ten books page by page and choose the first one.

## A. Mallow, the cuddly toy lamb who comes to life

**Who:** a soft-toy lamb that belongs to the child. When the book opens, Mallow wakes up: "*{name}*! It's digger day!" Mallow comes along to every job and is a cuddly toy again at bedtime. Mallow is gentle, curious and a little wobbly at first, and always wants a hug before a big job ("Hug first, then help!"). Mallow is brave with the child's help. Because Mallow is soft, the gentle comedy is toy comedy: Mallow gets squashed and bounces back, gets muddy on digger day and needs a wash, and the hard hat slips over Mallow's eyes.

**Look:** head about half of total height on a squat, cloud-edged fleece body. Stubby arms end in soft mitts (no hooves) that can hold things. Fleece is oatmeal cream `#F4E7D3` with scallop shading `#E6D3B8`. Face, ears, mitts and feet are cocoa velour `#8C6A55`. Inner ears are dusty rose `#E9B8B0` and cheeks `#F2A7A0`. Eyes are embroidered ovals `#2E2420` with a stitched highlight (never buttons). **Signature:** a hand-stitched yellow heart patch `#FFC845` with red running stitch `#E4483A` on the tummy; a cream fabric label with a red border on the left foot, where the child's name appears; and a left ear that flops lower than the right. Job clothes are toy-sized dress-up worn over the fleece. The heart and the foot label always stay visible.

**Name ideas:** **Mallow** (soft like a marshmallow), Tuffet, Crumble, Snowdrop.

**Refrain idea:** "Who can help? *{name}* can help!" The ending is always: "Mallow is a cuddly again. Night night, *{name}*."

**Friends:** Goose (a real goose, she: loud, kind and a bit bossy, carried over from the Book 1 draft), Crumbs (a scruffy puppy, he: always sniffing out crumbs), and Dabble (the child's rubber bath duck, they: a second toy that comes to life, tiny and brave).

**Why it works:** it turns "most kids have a teddy" into the whole idea, without a bear. It gives the child's name a place on the hero itself. Lambs and cuddly toys don't lean boy or girl. A small toy next to a capable child makes *the child* the one who helps. There's an obvious soft-toy product to sell later, with a real name label.

**Risks:** Lambie, the soft-toy lamb who comes to life in Disney's *Doc McStuffins* (unverified), is the closest existing character. So: no pink body, no tutu or bows, no cuddle catchphrase, and **no toy-hospital storyline** (make the Doctor book a people's clinic). The name on a toy is also a *Toy Story* echo (Andy's name on Woody's boot, unverified); that is a common idea, but we should know about it. Mallow may feel young to some 4–5-year-olds. Lambs tempt "baa" sound words, which we are avoiding.

## B. Nimble, the baby elephant

**Who:** a baby elephant who is surprisingly light on their feet. Nimble tiptoes, squeezes into small cabs and dances (no size jokes). Nimble is keen, warm and proud of a big memory, except that each day Nimble forgets one thing and the child remembers it. That gives a repeatable sentence frame: "Nimble never forgets… uh-oh! Where's my hard hat? *{name}* knows!" Toddlers love being the one who knows.

**Look:** a round calf with the head about half of total height, big low-set eyes, round cheeks and short thick legs with three pale toenails `#F3EDE6`. Two soft half-moon ears are smaller than Dumbo's and plain. The short trunk ends in a curl like a comma. Body is warm lilac-grey `#A7A2B8` with shading `#8E88A3`. Inner ears and cheeks are apricot `#F6B89C`. Eyes are `#2B2330` with a highlight. **Signature:** a fuzzy tuft of apricot-ginger baby hair `#E98F6B` on top of the head (real calves are hairy), plus the comma-curl trunk. Helmets have the tuft poking out, and the trunk holds each job's tool (hose, stethoscope, whistle, spanner).

**Name ideas:** **Nimble**, Pompom, Trundle.

**Refrain idea:** the "Nimble never forgets… *{name}* knows!" frame above. The ending is a trunk hug: "Trunk hug, side by side. Night night, *{name}*."

**Friends:** Goose (she), Crumbs (a puppy, he), and Tuck (a tortoise, they: slow and steady, and always arrives just in time).

**Why it works:** elephants are a nursery and comforter staple and don't lean boy or girl. No elephant owns the jobs niche. The silhouette works at any size, and the trunk gives a visual joke in every job (spraying at the fire, lifting on the building site). It's the best-scoring option.

**Risks:** Elmer dominates UK elephant picture books, so no patchwork, no multicolour and never "Elmer"-like names. Also avoid Babar's green suit and crown and Dumbo's giant ears or circus collar, and stay away from circus settings altogether. "Elephant" is a later word for toddlers than "dog" or "duck". Nimble has to be drawn toddler-sized so it fits in a digger cab. "Nimble" is also a UK bread brand (unverified), though that isn't one of our classes.

## C. Lollop, the lop-eared bunny

**Who:** a bouncy, eager bunny who hops ahead, then waits for the child. Lollop is a bit shy with new grown-ups, then brave. Big ears hear everything first. Refrain idea: "Listen… what can Lollop hear?"

**Look:** head about half of height, round body, big low-set eyes and a small nose `#E58A8A`. Long lop ears hang past the chin to the floor. Fur is honey-caramel `#D9A066`, with cream `#F7E6CC` muzzle, tummy and inner ears. **Signature:** the tip of the right ear is dark toffee `#9C6A3C`, as if dipped in paint, plus a cream pompom tail. Hats have slits so the ears flop out.

**Name ideas:** **Lollop** (how rabbits move), Bramble, Tuft, Hopscotch.

**Friends:** Goose (she), Burr (a hedgehog, he: prickly outside, soft inside), and Dab (a duckling, she).

**Why it works:** after the bear, the bunny is the most popular comfort toy and one of the most common picture-book animals. It's the "popular" choice you asked about, and no rabbit owns the jobs theme.

**Risks:** it's the most crowded lane: Miffy, Peter Rabbit, Bing, Nutbrown Hare, and Pip from Nosy Crow (Bizzy Bear's publisher). Miffy's owner has sued a lookalike rabbit before. A lop-eared plush look sits close to Jellycat's Bashful Bunny. Rabbits read as female. There is Easter clutter every spring. An older picture book about a toy rabbit called "Lollopy" may exist (unverified).

## D. Nuzzle, the koala joey

**Who:** a calm, cosy hugger who dozes off at funny moments. Refrain idea: "Is Nuzzle awake? Not yet! Wake up, Nuzzle, it's *{name}*'s big day!"

**Look:** head about 55% of height, round body, short arms. **Anti-bear cues:** huge round ears with white fluff `#F4F1EC` (far bigger than any bear's) and a big glossy dark nose `#3B3440`. Fur is smoky grey `#A8AEB5` with a cream chest `#EFE9E0`. A sprig of golden wattle `#F5C518` is tucked behind one ear. Never brown, and never called a bear.

**Name ideas:** **Nuzzle**, Wattle, Cobber (Australian for "mate"), Gumleaf.

**Friends:** Goose (she), Dig (a wombat, he: loves digging, made for the digger book), and Dab (a duckling, she).

**Why it works:** it's the closest thing to a teddy face without being a bear. Hugging is what koalas naturally do, and it's an open lane in UK pre-school books.

**Risks:** many people say "koala bear", so on a thumbnail it may look like the Bizzy Bear copy we are trying to avoid. One report said Nosy Crow has bought a superhero-koala series (unverified). There is also Blinky Bill. A sleepy character sits awkwardly in busy job books. Koalas are less familiar to UK two-year-olds. I rank it below the others for these reasons.

## E. Dimple, the baby hippo

**Who:** a jolly, splashy, big-hearted hippo who laughs a lot and loves water, so every book has a water moment (washing the fire engine, puddles, rain). Refrain idea: "Ready, Dimple? Ready, *{name}*? Ready!"

**Look:** barrel body, head about half of height with a wide round snout, tiny wiggly ears, nostril bumps and big eyes set high. Slate-violet body `#8E86A8`, lavender muzzle and tummy `#C8C1DA`, coral inner ears and cheeks `#F29C8B`. **Signature:** two dimples and two little white peg teeth when grinning, like a toddler's first teeth.

**Name ideas:** **Dimple**, Welly ("give it some welly!"), Burble, Paddle.

**Friends:** Nib (a tiny bird who rides on Dimple's back, she), Crumbs (a puppy, he), and Goose (she).

**Why it works:** it's an open lane with no big UK pre-school hippo lead. The round shape suits a cuddle, it doesn't lean boy or girl, and a big friend in a little digger is funny.

**Risks:** there must be **no weight jokes**, ever. Roly in Hey Duggee is a hippo friend character. It's less of a comforter than the lamb or the elephant. "Dimple" is also a Scotch whisky name (Haig Dimple, unverified) and a given name in some South Asian families. "Welly" rhymes with "Nellie".

## F. Nutmeg, the red panda cub

**Who:** a curious climber who wraps up in that big tail like a scarf when feeling wobbly. That's a gentle way to show a child how to calm down. Refrain idea: "Tail up, Nutmeg! Let's go, *{name}*!"

**Look:** round head about half of height, short round snout (not fox-pointed), and short chocolate legs and belly `#3A2320`. Fur is rust-orange `#D9692E` with a white face mask `#FFF6EC` and red-brown tear marks `#8A3B1E`. **Signature:** a ringed tail as long as the body, with rings in `#D9692E` and `#F2B27A`.

**Name ideas:** **Nutmeg** (also a football trick, which suits Book 1), Russet, Cinnamon, Toffee.

**Friends:** Goose (she), Crumbs (a puppy, he), and Tuck (a tortoise, they).

**Why it works:** it's fresh and very cute, stands out on Amazon in orange, and doesn't lean boy or girl. The tail is a comfort idea built into the character.

**Risks:** two-year-olds and grandparents may read it as a fox or a raccoon. The word "panda" suggests a bear. It is associated with *Turning Red* and Kung Fu Panda's Shifu (and Firefox's logo is a red panda, unverified). "Nutmeg" ends in "Meg", which is a child's name.

## Why not a puppy, mouse, cat or pig?

They are more popular, but each is already owned in exactly our space: PAW Patrol's puppies do jobs, Maisy the mouse goes to work, and Peppa owns pigs. A puppy doing jobs would get the same "copy" reaction as a bear. See the lanes table in the research paper.

## Name checks still to do

**What was checked:** only my general knowledge. This session's web-search budget (200) was used up before this step, and the search and library sites (DuckDuckGo, Bing, Open Library, Goodreads, WorldCat, UKIPO) are blocked by the proxy. The Google Books API refused requests (too many requests).

**What must happen before we commit to a name** (about an hour's work):

| Check | How |
|---|---|
| Existing characters | Web-search `"<name> the <species>"`, `"<name>" children's book <species>` and `"<name>" soft toy` |
| Books on sale | Amazon UK: search the name in Books > Children's, and in Toys |
| Trade marks | UKIPO and EUIPO: the name in classes 9 (apps), 16 (books), 28 (toys) and 41 (entertainment) |
| Clashes with children's names | ONS baby names (England and Wales): the name, and close sound-alikes (e.g. Mallow vs **Marlowe**, Nutmeg vs Meg, Lollop vs Lola) |
| How it sounds read aloud | Run it through our text-to-speech voices next to 20 common first names |

Names already ruled out in earlier work: Tiffin, Bobble, Bobbin, Pip, Kip, Scoot, Pebble, Fen, Otto and Ollie. Also avoid Teddy (a common boy's name), Woolly (*Woolly and Tig*), Lambie, Ellie and Nellie (elephant clichés), Puddle (Peppa's muddy puddles), Tumble (Tumble Tots) and Plum (UK slang for a fool).
