# Tiffin & Me — series bible (working draft)

> Working title and placeholder character, written for the prototype. They're original, and deliberately unlike Bizzy Bear: not a bear, a different look and a different series name. Treat them as a starting point for your own author and illustrator, not a finished brand. See the IP notes in `product-plan.md`.

## The idea in one line

Tiffin is a small, bouncy river otter who tries a new busy-day adventure in every book: football, the seaside, a train ride, a birthday, a trip to the moon. She always does it with her best friend: your child. The child is never drawn. They are 'you', the friend just off the page. The ball rolls towards the reader, and the child's own hand completes each big moment by working the mechanism. The printed board book is mass-produced. It has blank 'name spots' built into the art (a shirt back, bunting, a banner, a scoreboard, a plaque), and it reads naturally without the app. A parent scans the QR code on the back and types the child's name, then checks how the name sounds. The web app then reads the story aloud with the name woven in. It follows the book page by page, animates each mechanism, and shows the name appearing in those spots through the phone camera. Each title is named '[cheer or sound], {name}!' and ends at bedtime with the series signature. Real otters hold paws while they sleep so they never drift apart, so every book closes with 'Paw in hand, side by side. Night night, {name}.'

## The hero: Tiffin

- **Who:** Tiffin, a young river otter (eurasian otter pup).
- **Personality:** Bouncy, sunny and a little bit wobbly. She always passes and never hogs the ball. She cheers loudest for her friends. When things go wrong she says 'Uh-oh!' and tries again. Tiffin gets very excited, then very sleepy: otters nap a lot, so every day ends with a cosy snooze. She loves pebbles, splashing and doing everything together with her best friend, the child reading.
- **Catchphrase:** “Paw in hand, side by side!”
- **Pronouns:** she/her. The text almost never needs a pronoun. Always say 'Tiffin', so a toddler never mixes up who 'she' is and the child's name stays the only other name on the page.
- **Look:** Chunky toddler proportions, with the head about 40% of total height. Body: a rounded bean shape in warm otter brown #B0703F, with a long, thick tail that tapers to a point. Face and tummy: a heart-shaped muzzle and an oval tummy patch in cream #F7E6CC. Head: a wide rounded oval with two small round ears (inner ear #8A5530). Eyes: big round #2B1E16 with a white highlight dot. Nose: a rounded triangle in #3A2A20, with three thin whisker lines on each side in #3A2A20. Signature: a three-flick curly tuft of fur on top of the head in #8A5530. Paws are short and webbed, with #8A5530 pads. Football kit: red #E4483A shirt with a white #FFFFFF collar and white number 7, white shorts, red socks with white tops, black #2B2B2B boots with sunny-yellow #FFCB3D laces. Goalkeeper gloves when needed: #FFCB3D. Recurring prop in every book: a lucky blue-grey pebble #8FA3B8 with a white spot, hidden somewhere on each spread (a free search-and-find game). Draw everything as flat shapes with no outlines, or a 4px darker-brown outline. She must never look like a bear: there is always a long tail, whiskers and a slim, bendy body.
- **Originality check:** Checked by web search (Sept 2026). No otter or animal character called 'Tiffin' came up in children's books or TV. The nearest uses are unrelated. (1) 'Mr. Tiffin's Classroom', a Penguin Random House picture-book series where Tiffin is a human teacher's surname (https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/series/FFC/mr-tiffins-classroom-series/). (2) 'Tiffy', a pink bird in the German Sesamstrasse (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffy). 'Tiffin' is otherwise a common word: a Scottish chocolate traybake, and the Anglo-Indian word for a light lunch or lunchbox. It is not a toy or publishing brand. Well-known otters I checked so the design stays distinct: Sam Garton's 'I Am Otter', PB&J Otter (Peanut, Jelly, Butter), Emmet Otter, Tarka the Otter, and Otter and Portly from The Wind in the Willows (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Fictional_otters, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PB%26J_Otter). None is called Tiffin. Tiffin's head tuft, red kit, heart-shaped cream muzzle and lucky pebble keep her visually separate. Names I rejected: 'Bobble' (a hamster in CBeebies' Olobob Top, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olobob_Top); 'Bobbin' (a rabbit in Blinky Bill, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Blinky_Bill_characters, and 'bobbins' means rubbish in northern UK slang); 'Pip' (Nosy Crow's Pip and Posy, the same publisher as Bizzy Bear); 'Kip' (too close to Kipper); 'Scoot' (an airline brand); 'Pebble' (a smartwatch brand); 'Fen' (sounds like Finn, a popular child's name, which would confuse a personalised read); 'Otto' and 'Ollie' (common children's names). Tiffin is not a bear, not yellow, and has no Bizzy Bear design cues. Before launch, still run a formal UKIPO/EUIPO trade mark search in classes 9, 16, 28 and 41.

### How each mechanism works, in print and on screen

**Page 2 — Flap.** *In the book:* A die-cut board flap shaped like the front panel of the big red kit bag, hinged along the yellow zip line at the top. Lifting it reveals a folded red shirt seen from the back, with a blank white name panel above a big white number 1 and a pair of rolled white socks. The name panel carries a faint printed star, so it looks finished without the app. *On screen:* The child taps or drags the bag's front panel upwards. It flips open with a pop, and the red shirt wriggles up out of the bag and unfolds. The child's name then writes itself across the back of the shirt with a ding and a sparkle, and Tiffin claps.

**Page 3 — Wheel.** *In the book:* A rotating board disc sits behind a circular die-cut window in the middle of the page, and its thumb notch pokes out at the bottom page edge. Turning it shows Tiffin in three warm-up poses in turn: stretching up high, bending to touch her toes, and a star jump. *On screen:* The child drags in a circle on the wheel, or swipes. Tiffin cycles through stretch, bend and star jump, with a boing on each change. Each full turn makes the bunting flutter, and each flag shows one letter of the child's name, popping into place one flag at a time.

**Page 4 — Slider.** *In the book:* A football-shaped knob runs in a curved slot. The slot starts at Tiffin's foot (left), sweeps across the pitch, and ends at the bottom-right corner nearest the reader, as if the ball comes to you. Then the child slides it back again. *On screen:* The child drags the ball along a softly glowing track. It rolls and grows bigger as it comes 'to you', and lands with a thud. Sliding it back makes Tiffin's leg swing to kick it on, with a second thud. The crowd's banner with the child's name waves, and a small cheer plays on each pass.

**Page 5 — Pull-tab.** *In the book:* A red tab with a white arrow sticks out of the left page edge at about y=540. Pulling it slides the die-cut Tiffin figure diagonally up and left across the goal mouth, so her gloved paws cover the ball in the top-left corner. The ball is printed there, half-hidden until Tiffin arrives. *On screen:* The child drags the tab out to the left. Tiffin launches into a slow-motion dive with a whoosh, and the ball bounces off her gloves with a boing and drops into her arms. The crowd cheers, and the scoreboard with the child's name blinks.

**Page 6 — Slider.** *In the book:* The football knob starts on the penalty spot at the bottom centre. The child pushes it up a straight slot into the top-right corner of the net. A linked strip behind the page turns the scoreboard window from 0 to 1 as the ball goes in. *On screen:* The child pushes or flicks the ball upwards. It shoots into the top corner with a kick sound while Frog leaps the wrong way. The net bulges and ripples, and the scoreboard flips from 0 to 1 next to the child's name. Confetti bursts and the crowd cheers and claps. The name flashes three times on the scoreboard.

**Page 7 — Flap.** *In the book:* A flap shaped like a red cloth is draped over a tall shape on a white podium. Lifting it reveals a shiny gold cup with a cream plaque on the front, which is blank in print apart from a faint star. *On screen:* The child taps or drags the cloth away and it flutters off with a pop. Confetti pops and the cup sparkles. The child's name engraves itself onto the plaque with a ding, and everyone claps, including Goose and Frog.

### Rhythm and repetition

Word count is 143 words read aloud across text and after on all eight pages (hyphenated words count as one). With the six spoken prompts it is 183. Each spread has 13-22 words. The refrain 'Tip-tap, tip-tap, on we go!' (the sound of boots dribbling the ball) closes spreads 2, 3, 4 and 5. It becomes 'Goal, goal, goal!' at the climax (spread 6) and 'Tip-tap, tip-tap, hooray!' at the celebration (spread 7), then slows to 'Tip... tap... off to sleep we go.' on the bedtime page, so the book gets calmer as it ends. Parallel pairs toddlers can join in with: 'Tiffin has boots. Tiffin has a ball.' / 'Warm up, Tiffin! Warm up, {name}!' / 'Tiffin to {name}... thud!' then '{name} to Tiffin... thud!'. Whistles bookend the match: 'Peep! Kick-off!' on spread 4 and 'Peep, peep! Full time!' on spread 7. Sound words: thud, boing, whoosh, peep, hooray. Every prompt starts with a verb (Lift, Turn, Slide, Pull, Push, Lift). Name checks: {name's} appears on spread 2. {name} starts a sentence on spread 4 ('{name} to Tiffin... thud!') and sits mid-sentence on the cover and on spreads 4, 6 and 7. The name is spoken 11 times including one prompt, and appears inside the art on all six spreads plus the cover and the end page. The title's payoff line 'Goal, {name}!' comes back as the climax on spread 6. The child's two actions in the story, passing and scoring, are exactly what the real child does with the slider, so the child's own hand wins the game. Tiffin makes the save, so the child's moment stays the winning goal.

## Future titles

### Splash, {name}!
- **Setting:** A sunny seaside with rock pools, a sandcastle, gentle waves and an ice-blue sea for Tiffin to swim in, ending with a sandy walk home at sunset.
- **Mechanisms:** Wheel: waves roll in and out. Flap: lift the seaweed to find a crab in the rock pool. Slider: the spade digs a hole. Pull-tab: Tiffin dives off a rock, splash! Slider: the bucket tips out a sandcastle. Flap: open the picnic basket.
- **Name moments:** The name written in the wet sand with a stick (the app draws it stroke by stroke), the flag on top of the sandcastle, the stripy beach towel, the kite's tail. Text: 'Splash, {name}!', '{name's} sandcastle', 'Tiffin swims to {name}'.

### Toot toot, {name}!
- **Setting:** A little red steam train chuffing through green hills, over a bridge and through a tunnel to a picnic by a lake.
- **Mechanisms:** Slider: the train chuffs along the track. Wheel: the engine's wheels turn and the sheep in the field change. Pull-tab: the signal arm drops and the whistle goes toot. Flap: the dark tunnel opens to show the sunny side. Slider: the level-crossing gate lifts. Flap: the picnic hamper.
- **Name moments:** The engine's brass nameplate ('The {name} Express'), the train ticket, the station sign, the picnic blanket label. Text: 'Toot toot, {name}!', 'All aboard, {name}!'.

### Happy birthday, {name}!
- **Setting:** A garden party with balloons, games and a big cake. It is culture-neutral: no specific foods or traditions, just friends, games and cake.
- **Mechanisms:** Flap: unwrap the present. Wheel: pass-the-parcel goes round the circle. Slider: a balloon floats up. Pull-tab: the candles puff out. Slider: the party hat pops on Tiffin. Flap: open the party bag.
- **Name moments:** Piped in icing on the cake, the 'HAPPY BIRTHDAY {name}' banner, a badge showing the child's age (optional age field), the gift tag. This is the strongest gifting title commercially. The app can also set the number of candles.

### Blast off, {name}!
- **Setting:** A cardboard-box rocket becomes a real one. It flies past twinkly planets to a gentle, glowing moon, then floats home to bed. It is a perfect bedtime read.
- **Mechanisms:** Slider: 5-4-3-2-1 blast off, the rocket rises. Wheel: the planets orbit. Flap: open the moon-crater hatch. Pull-tab: the moon buggy bumps along. Wheel: stars spin into a constellation. Slider: the rocket parachutes home.
- **Name moments:** Painted on the side of the rocket, on the flag planted on the moon, on the space helmet, and as stars that join up into the child's first letter (animated in the app). Text: 'Blast off, {name}!', '{name's} rocket'.

### Beep beep, {name}!
- **Setting:** Digger day: Tiffin and friends build a new playground with a digger, a crane, a cement mixer and a dumper truck, then play on it at the end.
- **Mechanisms:** Pull-tab: the digger scoops. Wheel: the cement mixer turns. Slider: the crane lifts the slide into place. Flap: the hard-hat box. Slider: the dumper tips. Flap: the curtain comes off the finished playground.
- **Name moments:** A sticker on the hard hat, the digger door, and the sign on the finished playground ('{name's} Park', which uses the possessive naturally), plus the ribbon at the opening. Text: 'Beep beep, {name}!'.

## Writing personalised titles — house rules

- Series frame: every title is called '[cheer or sound], {name}!' (Goal, Splash, Toot toot, Blast off...). Every book has a cover, exactly 6 spreads with one mechanism each, and a bedtime end page. Every book ends with the signature 'Paw in hand, side by side. Night night, {name}.'
- Word budget: 8-25 words per spread (text plus after), sentences of 7 words or fewer, present tense, and 1-2 sound words per spread that the app can synthesise (whistle, kick, thud, boing, pop, clap, cheer, ding). One refrain repeats on at least 4 of the 6 spreads, changes at the climax, and slows down on the last page.
- The child's hand is the story's hand. The child's in-story actions (find, pass, score, lift, launch) are exactly what the mechanism does. Prompts start with an action verb (Lift, Turn, Slide, Pull, Push), are 8 words or fewer, and may include {name} at most once.
- Never gender the child. No he, she, his, her, boy, girl, son, daughter, prince, princess or 'little man'. Use {name}, 'you', 'your' and 'we'. Avoid pronouns for Tiffin in the text as well, so a toddler never has to work out who 'she' means.
- Make no assumptions about family (say 'grown-ups' or 'friends', never mummy, daddy or granny), ability (Tiffin runs and jumps; the child passes, scores, finds and cheers), culture or religion (no specific festivals, foods or prayers), or appearance. The child is never drawn. They appear as the reader's point of view and as name spots in the art.
- Name frequency: {name} goes on the cover and in the final line, once or twice per spread in the text, and three times at most including the prompt. Every book uses {name} at the start of a sentence at least once, mid-sentence at least once, and {name's} at least once.
- Name in the art: at least 4 spreads, always on objects that naturally carry a name (shirt back, bunting, banner, scoreboard, plaque, sign, nameplate, sand). In the printed book these spots are blank shapes with a faint star, so the page looks finished without the app. The app overlays the name in the right perspective, animating it in with a ding.
- Put the name where its length doesn't break the rhythm: at the end of a phrase or after a comma when speaking to the child ('Goal, {name}!', 'Warm up, {name}!', 'Night night, {name}.'). Never put it in the middle of a strict rhyming line. Read every line aloud with a test set of names: Bo, Zoë, Kit, Hope, Siobhán, Oluwaseun, Maximilian, Anna-Sophia, Xiao Ming.
- Long names (more than 10 characters or more than 4 syllables): ask the grown-up for the name the child is usually called ('What do you call them at home?') and use it in both audio and art. Name spots shrink to a minimum letter size, then break onto two lines at a space or hyphen. Bunting grows to 12 flags, beyond that it uses the nickname. Never cut a name off with an ellipsis.
- Possessives are generated in code, in UK style with 's even after an s: Amara's, James's, Alex's, Mary-Kate's, Zoë's. The name is always spoken inside a fully synthesised sentence, never spliced in as a separate clip, so the voice around it sounds natural.
- Names that are also words (Hope, Joy, Grace, Rose, River, Sky, Summer, Storm, Hunter, Will, Sunny, Autumn, Blue, Kit, Goose): check every line for accidental double meanings or clumsy echoes, such as 'Look at the sky, Sky!' or 'Where is Kit's kit?'. Write a swap-in alternative for any line that trips. Never put the name straight after a helping verb ('can', 'will') or before a noun it could be read as describing.
- If the child's name matches or sounds like a character's name (Tiffin, Tiffany, Tiff, Goose, Frog), ask for a nickname, or have the narrator say 'Tiffin the otter' on those pages so the two are never confused. Minor characters are named by species (Goose, Frog), never with human names.
- Twins and siblings: support up to 3 names, joined UK-style without an Oxford comma ('Amara and Zak', 'Amara, Zak and Li'). Every line containing {name} needs its own plural version, because verbs and nouns change: 'Where are Amara and Zak's shirts?'. Duplicate the name spots in the art (two shirts, two plaques). Rotate which sibling is named first on each spread, and share the big moment ('Goal, Amara and Zak!').
- Print fallback: every personalised line has a print version for the physical book that uses you/your or simply drops the name, for example 'Where is your shirt?', 'Tiffin to you... thud!', 'Goal! Goal, goal, goal!'. The printed book must read naturally with no phone in the room.
- Pronunciation: before reading, the grown-up hears the name spoken. If it's wrong, they can pick from alternative pronunciations, type a 'sounds like' respelling (for example 'shiv-AWN'), or record the name in their own voice as a last resort. Store the pronunciation in phonetic form. The narrator says the name at the same pace and warmth as the rest of the line and never spells it out.
- Speech-safe text: keep display text and spoken text in separate fields. Avoid ALL CAPS in spoken text, because some voices spell capitals out letter by letter; mark emphasis instead. Use '...' for dramatic pauses before a payoff ('Ready... steady...'). Spell sound words the way they should sound.
- Name rendering: show names exactly as typed, including accents, apostrophes and hyphens (Zoë, De'Andre, Siân, McKenzie). Never force them into capitals on shirts or scoreboards in a way that makes them hard to read. Use a fallback font that covers non-Latin scripts. Offer a filter that warns but never blocks, because real names can collide with rude words.
- Privacy: child names stay on the device by default (UK GDPR and the ICO Children's Code). No account is needed to read. Names never go into URLs, analytics or third-party text-to-speech without clear consent from the grown-up.
- Tone: opponents are friendly and clap at the end. Any 'uh-oh' moment is resolved on the same spread, and there is no scary danger and no sulking. Tiffin is never unkind and always shares the ball. The child's big moment (the winning goal, the blast-off, the candles) is never taken by Tiffin.
- Replay value: hide Tiffin's lucky blue-grey pebble on every spread for a search-and-find game. Keep the refrain easy to chant, so by the third read a toddler says it before the narrator does.

HOW OURS DIFFER.
(1) Titles: every Bizzy Bear title is 'Bizzy Bear: [role, vehicle or place]', e.g. Football Player, Aeroplane Pilot, Train Driver, Building Site. None of our titles contains a job title, the word 'Bizzy' or a bear. Ours are always '[sound], {name}!', so each printed title is also unique to the child. 'Beep beep, {name}!' matched no Bizzy Bear title.
(2) Format: Bizzy Bear uses about five sliders plus rhyming verse, with the child helping the bear. Ours has six spreads, each with a different kind of mechanism (flap, push-button, pull-tab, wheel, slider, flap). The text is an unrhymed chant with a refrain that changes at the climax and slows down at bedtime. The child is named as Tiffin's co-driver, appears as 'you' (never drawn), has their name built into the art, and the book ends with our own signature, 'Paw in hand, side by side.'
(3) Hero and look: an otter in orange hi-vis and a yellow hard hat, not a yellow bear. The digger is red (not yellow) and the friends are Goose and Frog.
(4) The digger story itself: Bizzy Bear 'Let's Get to Work!/Building Site' is a bear driving a digger, bulldozer and truck around a building site. In ours, Tiffin and the child build a playground with Goose's dumper, Frog's mixer and a crane, and it opens as '{name's} Park'.
(5) Phrases kept out of Book 2: 'Let's get to work', 'off we go', 'let's go and play', 'Playtime Park', 'Fun Park', 'building site'. From other digger books: no 'dig, dig' chant (Emma Garcia's 'Tip Tip Dig Dig') and no 'Dig! Dump! Beep!' trio (John Deere Kids).

**Done:** Book 1's refrain was changed to “Tip-tap, tip-tap, on we go!”. Original flag: its refrain 'Tip-tap, tip-tap, off we go!' contains the Bizzy Bear title 'Off We Go!'. The phrase is too common to be protected, but for a clean distance consider 'Tip-tap, tip-tap, on we go!'. I did not change Book 1.

Then set p1 and p3 to 'beep' and p4 to 'rumble'. I did not edit sfx.js.
